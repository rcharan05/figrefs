Three observations.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The learned models win on near recall.} The rule recovers 68\% of near implicit sentences; the
        two-model ensemble recovers 78\% and the three-model ensemble 85\%, at a near precision that drops
        only from 0.74 to 0.66--0.68.
  \item \textbf{Far references remain unsolved.} The best system finds 3 of 112. When a model does select a
        far sentence it is often right (3 of 5 for the three-model ensemble), which shows that the models are
        appropriately cautious, not that they understand far references.
  \item \textbf{Out-of-fold gains over the rule are small.} Between $+0.004$ and $+0.024$ F1 on the training
        papers; the clearer test of the models is the sealed test below.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The sealed test}
\label{sec:test}

SPAN, anchored), the threshold ($t=-3.6$, from the out-of-fold scores of the training papers), and the comparison
(\bof{}). The final C0L and SPAN models, trained on all 15

\paragraph{The pre-registered result.} The ensemble scores \textbf{F1 0.694 against 0.622} for \bof{}, a gain
of $+0.071$. The gain is recall: 35 instead of 24 of the
46 near implicit sentences, and 3 far references, all correct, where the rule finds none. Precision is almost
unchanged (0.791 against 0.814). Each model alone gains less over the rule ($+0.017$ and $+0.045$); the
combination with the anchored decision rule gains the most.

\paragraph{Comparison with published work.} The best published result on GS1 is F1 0.66, measured on the full
article set. Our rule and our models are above it on our training papers under out-of-fold evaluation (0.752 and
0.766 against 0.659 for our re-implementation of the same method), and the ensemble is above it on the test
papers (0.694 against 0.667). We claim the best reported results on \figsum{} GS1. We do not claim a general
state of the art for implicit figure references: the benchmark is small, rarely used, and limited to
biomedicine.
