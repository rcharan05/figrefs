\begin{abstract}
\noindent
A figure in a research paper is discussed in many places: in sentences that name it (``as shown in Fig.~3B''), and in
sentences that talk about what it shows without naming it. We build a tool that, given a directory of papers,
returns for every figure all of this text. Sentences that name a figure (\emph{explicit} references) are found
with a hand-written grammar, which reaches a sentence-level F1 of 0.93 on arXiv papers and 0.85 on held-out
bioRxiv papers. Sentences that do not name the figure (\emph{implicit} references) are the research problem.
We study it on \figsum{} GS1, the only public benchmark with human labels for implicit figure references
(18 biomedical articles, 91 figures; best published F1 0.66).

We first show that a carefully filtered positional rule, \bof{}, already beats the published best
(F1 0.752 against 0.659 on the same 15 papers). We then report a long series of attempts to beat this rule with
learned models: feature models, sequence models, small and large language models as judges, free labels mined
from 6{,}049 papers, a two-stage curriculum, data augmentation, a pairwise cross-encoder (DeBERTa-v3-large), a
whole-paper span tagger (ModernBERT-large), and text extracted from the figure image. Most of these did not
help, and we report them all. What did work was (i) training on every real figure--sentence pair,
(ii) reading long stretches of the paper at once, and (iii) a better way of turning scores into a set of
sentences, which we call \emph{anchoring}. In a pre-registered test on three unseen papers, opened once, an
ensemble of the two transformers scores F1 0.694 against 0.622 for \bof{} ($+0.071$). The gain is recall on implicit sentences near a citation; references far from any citation
remain largely unsolved. A sanity check on a computer-science paper suggests that the model transfers outside
biomedicine, with caveats that we state. The tool, the trained models and all experiment and training code are released at
\url{https://github.com/rcharan05/figrefs}.
\end{abstract}
