\section{Parsing Routes}
\label{app:parsing}
Every input format is converted into the same article model (Section~\ref{sec:pipeline}).
\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.2em,style=nextline,itemsep=3pt]
  \item[Structured XML (JATS)] The cleanest input. Sections, paragraphs, captions and figure citations are
        read directly from the markup.
  \item[PDF through GROBID] GROBID 0.9.1 \citep{lopez2009grobid} produces structured XML from the PDF. We take
        figures, captions and paragraphs from its output, rebuild section labels, re-split sentences with our
        own splitter, and keep figure citations from both GROBID's reference tags and our grammar.
  \item[PDF through our own parser] A layout parser built on PyMuPDF that groups lines into paragraphs, finds
        headings and captions, filters page furniture (running headers, side panels, icon glyphs), and stops at
        the reference list. It needs no external service and is what the command-line tool uses by default.
  \item[\LaTeX{} source] Figures are numbered as \LaTeX{} numbers them, and \verb|\ref| commands to figure
        labels become explicit references.
\end{description}
Sentences are split by an abbreviation-aware splitter, so that ``Fig.~3'' or ``et al.'' does not end a
sentence.

\section{Other Approaches in Detail}
\label{app:other}
This appendix describes the approaches summarised in Table~\ref{tab:other} (Section~\ref{sec:othertrials}).

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.2em,style=nextline,itemsep=3pt]
  \item[Feature models] Logistic regression and gradient-boosted trees over hand-built features (distance and
        direction to the citation, paragraph and section, cue phrases, similarity to the caption, coreference
        links), including a stacked variant. They essentially relearned the rule, and they were fragile: when
        the text came from PDFs instead of XML, a model trained on XML features degraded sharply.
  \item[Linear-chain CRF] Annotators tend to mark contiguous runs of sentences. A conditional random field
        \citep{lafferty2001crf} labels the block of sentences around a citation jointly and learns how likely a
        run is to continue or stop. With so few training papers it did not learn better transitions than the
        fixed \pmk{1} rule.
  \item[Text tiling] In the spirit of TextTiling \citep{hearst1997texttiling}, extend outward from each
        citation for as long as the word overlap between neighbouring sentences stays above a threshold.
        Word overlap is too weak a signal: a follow-up such as ``This effect was abolished by \ldots'' shares
        few words with the sentence it continues.
  \item[Anaphora continuation] Follow sentences that begin with ``These results'', ``This'', ``Such'' and
        similar back to a citing sentence. Slightly worse than the rule.
  \item[Small language models as judges] Local models of 1.5 to 3 billion parameters were asked whether a
        sentence is about a given figure. Their ranking of near sentences reached an AUC of at most 0.708.
        Letting such a judge extend \bof{} to distance 2--3 gave $+0.014$ on development papers but was not
        significant on the held-out papers of that protocol.
  \item[``Figure-talk'' classifier] A classifier trained, without any \figsum{} labels, to recognise prose that
        describes a figure: on 200 bioRxiv papers, a sentence is positive if it cites a figure, and the
        citation is masked out. On \figsum{} it ranked near sentences \emph{worse than chance} (AUC 0.46), and
        a tuned selection of far sentences based on it chose to add nothing.
  \item[Caption-similar far sentences] Adding even the single far sentence most similar to the caption costs
        0.059 F1; only four of the added sentences are gold.
  \item[Frontier LLMs as annotators] In a separate test on six papers, two proprietary LLMs used as zero-shot
        annotators agreed with the gold at a human-like level ($\kappa\approx0.73$) and were the only method
        with real precision on far sentences (about 0.32), but their F1 was about 0.05 below \bof{}. Since
        the tool must run locally, they were excluded from the tool and from label creation by design.
  \item[Embedding retrievers] Dense sentence embeddings (BGE, SPECTER2, mpnet) and a neural reranker added
        nothing over TF-IDF similarity for selecting sentences by their similarity to the caption.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Early stopping (SPAN\_ES).} Validation every half epoch on the three former test papers; metric:
average precision smoothed over the last two checks; no stop before epoch 3; stop after four checks without
a new best; best weights restored.

\paragraph{Decision rule of the registered ensemble.} Mean of the C0L and SPAN logits; minus the mean over
the figure's citing sentences (or the median over all its sentences if it is never cited); select if the
result is at least $-3.6$; always include the citing sentences.

\paragraph{Figure text (SPAN\_VIS).} Qwen2-VL-2B-Instruct, greedy decoding with a repetition penalty of 1.15.
Header: caption, then ``Figure text:'' with the OCR output, then ``Figure shows:'' with the description, up to
400 tokens.

\section{Repository}
The code and the instructions to reproduce every table are at \repourl. The three trained models used by the tool (C0L, SPAN and SPAN\_ES, about 6\,GB in total) are at \modelurl. In brief:
\texttt{figrefs/} is the tool; \texttt{demo/} runs the learned models on new papers and builds the result
viewer; \texttt{eval/} holds the benchmark code, the reproduction of the published baselines and the earlier
experiments; \texttt{eval/figsum/run1/} holds the training scripts of every transformer run, the offline
scorer and the sealed-test plan and script; \texttt{scripts/} fetches data; and \texttt{report/} holds this
document. The \texttt{README} describes every file.
